SyncInfra

A site-tracking platform for office fit-out contractors that ties daily progress, materials, and delays together, and explains why a handover date moved.

Role
Lead developer, team of 2
Built
Sep – Oct 2026
Stack
TypeScript · Fastify · Postgres · React

Private repository. Screenshots use the demo seed data, with company names replaced.

01Problem

On fit-out projects, progress, material stock, and the programme live in separate spreadsheets, chats, and meetings. Delays surface late, nobody can say which task caused them, claimed progress gets mixed up with verified progress, and material runs short without warning.

02Solution

One record runs floor → job → area → stage, and installation progress and material consumption are the same dataset. Only work signed off by an independent auditor counts. Every write recomputes the forecast and pushes a slipped date down the chain, so every delay has a named root cause.

03How it works

  1. ReportSite engineer claims progress on a stage
  2. AuditAuditor signs off, cuts, or rejects it
  3. EventThe write is logged and a job is queued
  4. RecomputeA worker recomputes the project's rollups
  5. RippleThe forecast shifts every dependent stage
  6. ActDashboards raise flags, shortages, and order-by dates
  • Business rules live in Postgres itself: CHECK constraints, foreign keys, and triggers. Computed tables refuse any write that doesn't come from the recompute pipeline.
  • Audit independence is enforced by triggers: whoever claims progress can't approve it, the approver can't report to the claimant (checked up the reporting chain), and nobody can clear a flag raised against themselves.
  • Event-driven recompute: each write lands in a transactional event log and queues a pg-boss job; a worker recomputes completion, forecasts, and delay chains. About 150 ms median from write to screen, measured locally on a 504-stage project.
  • Capability-based access: every API entry point goes through one deny-by-default permission check that logs denials, and screens a role can't use are never drawn.
  • Every write records who and when; the API refuses to start against a database that's behind its migrations; and a pure engine package with enforced boundaries holds the scheduling logic.

04Features 16

  • Sites overviewProgress, forecast against target, material at risk, blockers, and a setup checklist per site.
  • Site setup gateA new site stays locked until its bill of quantities and drawings are uploaded.
  • Floors and flowsEach floor as a room layout, a job-by-job matrix, or room by room.
  • Stage drawerEvery trade's sub-steps, claimed versus approved, with the auditor's sign-off form.
  • Delay chainsWhy the handover date moved: root causes and the days they add, stage by stage.
  • FlagsAuto-raised problems that can only be cleared with a stated reason.
  • Progress logEvery claim and what became of it, filterable by state, trade, and person.
  • MaterialsBill of quantities by trade with lead times, a sample tracker, and a materials log per floor.
  • DeliveryPurchase orders through five delivery states, with goods received.
  • Shortage deskShortages from site and from the system, resolved as a transfer or an order.
  • Outsourced worksExecution vendors by trade and floor, with inspections and rejections.
  • Team and hierarchyRole slots, assignments from director to site, and the chain of command.
  • The programmeConfirm suggested dependency links, or draw flow and parallel arrangements.
  • People directoryCompany-wide staff, invitations, password resets, availability, and deactivation.
  • Vendors and credit notesMaterial and crew vendors with lead times and capacity, and credit notes nearing expiry.
  • Sign-inPassword login, emailed invitation and reset links, and a person switcher for demos.
